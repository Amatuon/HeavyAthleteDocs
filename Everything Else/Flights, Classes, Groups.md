# I finally got comfortable with Classes and Flights why are you adding a third term to the mix?

In the trivial case a Class, Flight and Group are all equivalent. Which is where a lot of confusion stems from. On Heavy Athlete we use the terms to handle different problems that arise.

First is the problem of which rankings to put scores into. This is handled by Classes. It doesn't matter what happened on the field if an athlete is in the "Amateur Men" class then their scores go into the Amateur Men ranking.

The second problem is we often score multiple classes against each other on the field. The two most common cases are "Pro / Am", where the professionals and amateurs are throwing against each other and there is a single podium, and mixed masters classes. You may have split every Master into their correct age class for rankings but you have them all throw in one flight and score them as 1 group because you are only handling prize support for three athletes not three per age group. (This case is so common that we have added pseudo-classes, don't worry about that term its not on the quiz, called Master's Men and Master's Women. You can simply drop athletes here and let us sort them into the correct age groups for rankings.)

The mixed master's scenario also reveal our third problem. Master's Classes often get mixed with Lightweights since they throw the same implements. In this case typically the two sets of athletes are traveling together during the day and throwing together but they are scored separately. Flights handle the scoring separation but they don't offer a good solution to the underlying reason why we make Master's and Lightweights throw together so often.

The real reason is typically a manpower issue. Games have finite numbers of judges. This is what groups is attempting to solve. Groups allow you to group one or more flights together and and restrict the total signups. Think of your total number of groups on each day as the number of judges you have for that day.

Most judges I have met are fairly comfortable with about 12-15 athletes. Some can handle ~20. Most start to complain when the group they are working with gets above that number. Let me illustrate how to handle this with an example.

I as the AD know that I have 3 Judges available for my one day comp. Let's call my three judges Alice, Bob, and Carol. Alice is very experienced as a judge and a ~~real taskmaster~~ really efficient judge capable of working with larger groups of athletes. I am giving her my largest group of Athletes Amateur Men. I am going to allow 25 signups for this.

Let's break down what we see here. The Group is named Amateur Men, the Flight? box is checked, then is no Parent Group. The name can be anything but it is shown to athletes during signups so it would be good to pick one that is descriptive. If Flight? is checked it means that Athletes will see this as an option when signing up for the game. We will cover cases where it is useful to not have this checked in a moment but this is our simple example. Since this is still the simple example Parent Group is safe to ignore. 

Next we have Start Date and End Date. Since this a single day game there is nothing to do here. I will also cover an example where this can and should be changed later. 

Next we have our most important features Max Athletes, Classes, and Registrations. Max Athletes is the maximum number of Athletes that are allowed to sign up for this specific group. Since this Group is also a Flight and a single Class all three terms apply here when you are trying to reason about it. Next lets look closer at Registrations. This shows the current number of athletes signed up in this group. The progress bar will fill in as athletes sign up for a quick visual on capacity left. Last but certainly not least we have Classes. Clicking the green + icon will let you add as many classes as you want to a group while clicking the red x will remove that class from this group. Heavy Athlete has no way of knowing what the group name "Amateur Men" means. In this case its pretty obvious but you can type anything in there so "Open Men", "Amateur A", "Youngings" and more are all valid group names and we have no way of figuring out who you intend to let sign up for this group. However we do know a lot about our athletes, we have their gender, and age. We restrict which classes an athlete can sign up for to only valid classes for them and we can do the same for groups. A woman will not be shown the option to sign up for "Amateur Men" because there are no valid classes for her in the class list. We will revisit this later in some of the more advanced scenarios.

Last section there is a Descriptions box. The beauty of the group system is we are forcing most of the work of sorting everyone into the correct spot onto the athletes. It can be tricky to have a name that is descriptive enough to explain to everyone where they should go. (Its not to hard in this case since it's the simple case but whatever) The Public Description just like it says twice is visible to athletes during signup. You can use this to steer people towards where they should sign up something like "This group is for Amateurs only if you want to play against the Pros select the Pro / Am group." Then we have Private Notes this as it says is only visible to you. I don't know what you want to put here but it is a notepad available to you for planning. An option for planning is say "Alice's Group" so you don't forget.

First I've made a new group named Masters and Lightweight Men and I have NOT made it a flight. Next I have set the Parent Group for both Masters Men and Lightweight Men to our new group. After hitting update you can see that they are now contained by the card for our new group. Last thing our new group has a Max Athletes set to 15.

So what does this mean? Why doesn't our new group have any selected classes? Is there a really simple analogy for this? The group system is based on folders in a computer. Just like a folder can contain a mix of other folders and files, a group can contain other groups and Athletes. When an Athlete signs up for a flight they are added to that folder. If their folder becomes full then no new Athletes can be added. In a similar argument if the outermost folder is full then we also don't have room in any of its subfolders. Alright analogy over lets break this down concretely. Masters and Lightweight Men doesn't need any classes because no one can sign up for it since it isn't a flight (You can add classes to it if you really want to but if its not a flight they don't do anything). Then our current Max Athletes can be interpreted like this. Masters and Lightweight Men can have at most 15 Athletes up to 8 of which can be masters and 8 of which can lightweights. Lets say all 8 master's spots fill up before any lightweights sign up. No more masters will be allowed to sign up since their flight is full. Also only 7 lightweights will be able to sign up now. The eighth one will be blocked because while there is still room in their flight the parent group will be full with 8 masters and 7 lightweights. Effectively we have guaranteed 7 slots for each class and forced them to race for the final shared spot. This may or may not be what we intended. Here are some other settings and there outcomes. If all three of the groups are set to 15 then none of the spots are "safe" its a race for all 15 as in if 15 lightweights sign up before the first master than no masters can sign up. Another option is 15 max in total, 10 max masters, 5 max lightweight. In this case there are no races and all the spots are "safe" but there isn't really a big difference between this and option and not grouping the two flights. The last option I'll cover is 15 max in total, 5 max masters, 5 max lightweight. This is very similar to the last case all the spots are "safe" but there isn't really a reason to do it this way. If your child groups add up to less than the max in the parent group then the parent group fill criteria cannot be met. 

Let's sum that all up with some simple rules. 
1. Athlete signups count towards the cap of their selected Flight and all parent Groups. 
2. Groups prevent new signups when they fill up or any of their ancestors fill up. 

That's it that's all the logic.

## Alright that's information dense lets summarize everything.

Classes, Flights, and Groups are three similar words that all tackle different problems on Heavy Athlete. Classes for Rankings, Flights for Game Scores, and Groups for managing signups. 

1. Athlete signups count towards the cap of their selected Flight and all parent Groups. 
2. Groups prevent new signups when they fill up or any of their ancestors fill up. 


### One last less common use case

Lets say you are running multiple events at the same venue at the same time. Hypothetically a Strongman competition, a Stone Lifting meet, and a Highland Games. While it is possible to run and manage all three through Heavy Athlete as separate games it is also now much easier to run them all on the same page. The benefit to this is that all the scores are available in one place for spectators bouncing between events.

### Other Crazy Powerful things you can do. (But probably shouldn't)

A group can have as many children as you want. And child groups are just groups so they can have as many children as you want meaning the rabbit hole can go really deep (Eventually the display breaks).  This lets you create really powerful conditions but I don't recommend it. It becomes difficult to track the logic when you start working this way and may lead to unintended effects. You might be tempted to create a cycle of groups where one group is its own parent or ancestor. While this is technically possible with this system it is not allowed. There is a check in place to prevent the formation of a cycle but I don't recommend that you stress test my implementation. If you manage to create a cycle of groups then they wont display for you modify anymore due to how the display logic works.
